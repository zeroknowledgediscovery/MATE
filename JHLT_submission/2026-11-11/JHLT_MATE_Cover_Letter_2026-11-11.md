**Re: Brief Communication submission — “Non-fatal Major Adverse Transplant Episodes After Lung Transplantation: An EHR-Based Feasibility Study”**

Dear Dr. Rogers:

On behalf of my coauthors, I am pleased to submit our manuscript, “Non-fatal Major Adverse Transplant Episodes After Lung Transplantation: An EHR-Based Feasibility Study,” for consideration as a Brief Communication in The Journal of Heart and Lung Transplantation. We appreciated your encouraging response to Dr. Edward Cantu’s earlier inquiry about this topic and, in particular, your suggestion that we consider its applicability beyond the United States.

One-year survival, although essential, does not adequately distinguish uncomplicated recovery from survival accompanied by sustained morbidity. We propose non-fatal Major Adverse Transplant Episode (MATE) as a transparent, consequence-based composite of retransplantation, prolonged index hospitalization, late dialysis, oxygen dependence, and recurrent unplanned admissions. Using Epic COSMOS data, we evaluated ascertainment in a cohort of 27,469 lung transplant recipients. Among 27,031 recipients with state attribution, 8,995 (33.28%) met non-fatal MATE criteria. A hierarchical beta-binomial analysis identified geographic heterogeneity beyond sampling variation, while explicitly acknowledging that these estimates are not adjusted for patient case mix or center-level factors.

In response to your suggestion, we discuss the potential use of a common recovery endpoint across international lung transplantation systems, alongside the need to harmonize ascertainment, account for differences in coding and healthcare practices, and validate the measure before comparing programs. We position this analysis as a feasibility study—not a validated quality-ranking system—and describe planned prospective validation in the NIH PROMISE Study Lung Transplant Consortium, including comparisons with patient-reported outcomes. Reliable mortality ascertainment will be necessary before extending the measure to MATE-free survival.

We believe the manuscript will interest JHLT readers seeking outcomes that complement survival with clinically meaningful post-transplant recovery. Thank you for considering our submission.
